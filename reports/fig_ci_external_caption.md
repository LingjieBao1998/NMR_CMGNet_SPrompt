# Figure caption — 90% confidence intervals on the external test set

**Figure X. Confidence intervals of the XGBoost–CReSS classifier on the external test set (n = 4,502; 64 positives, τ = 0.50).**
(A) Every external-test spectrum is one circle, projected into the CReSS embedding UMAP and coloured by the width of the 90% bootstrap confidence interval of its calibrated class probability (logarithmic colour scale running from the grey used for confident negatives to the dark orange of uncertain predictions). False positives are drawn as diamonds and the single false negative as a star, with the raw model probability printed next to each error.
(B) The same intervals for the 64 positives and the 4 FP, with molecules sorted along x and the calibrated probability on the y axis (log scale). Whiskers are the 5th–95th percentiles of the Platt calibration map refitted on 200 stratified bootstrap resamples of the validation split and re-applied to each molecule. The dashed line is the decision threshold τ = 0.50, which corresponds to 0.0235 on the calibrated probability scale because the calibrator is fitted on the strongly imbalanced validation split.

Intervals are wide where the decision is uncertain: the median width is 0.068 for true positives and 4 × 10⁻⁹ for confidently predicted negatives. Two of the four FP (#763, #4201) have intervals that straddle the decision threshold, while the other two (#3898, #3460) are confidently wrong. The single FN (#12) is also confidently wrong — its interval (9 × 10⁻⁵ – 6 × 10⁻⁴) lies far below the threshold — consistent with a genuine coverage gap rather than a borderline draw.
